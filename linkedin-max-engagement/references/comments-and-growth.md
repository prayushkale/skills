## Why comments matter
- Comments are a separately predicted "active" objective in LinkedIn's ranker [P]. AuthoredUp estimates a comment carries about 2× the weight of a like [S].
- Conversation depth helps. Threads where readers and the author reply to each other reached up to 2.4× more in AuthoredUp's data [S].
- Your comments on other people's posts put your name and headline in front of their audience. This is the main discovery engine for small accounts.

## Commenting on others' posts
**Rules**
- Hand-write every comment. LinkedIn hides automated comments from "Most Relevant" [P, Mar 2026].
- Write 15+ words, usually 2-4 sentences. Add a specific experience, data point, nuance, or question.
- No pitches, no links, no "Great post!", no "Thanks for sharing!".
- Respectful disagreement with evidence often gets the most replies.

**Volume and timing**
- Estimates range from 3-5 substantive comments a day to 10-15 [B, conflicting]. Some creators warn that 20-50 shallow comments can hurt. Choose quality.
- Comment early on posts that are gaining traction. A vendor-cited study of 657k comments found that a comment in the first 30 minutes got about 4× the impressions of the same comment a day later [B, unverified].
- Commenting for 15-30 minutes around your own posting time is a common creator habit [B]. The "+21% reach" claim attached to it is unverified.

**Targets**
- About 5-10 creators in your niche whose audience you want.
- Peers at your level, who are likely to reply and reciprocate.
- Posts where you have genuine expertise to add.

**Templates**
- "[Specific point] is the part most people skip. When we [did X], [result]."
- "Agree on [point]. The exception I've seen: [context] → [what happened]. Have you run into that?"
- "We tested this with [N] [clients/teams]. [Outcome]. The surprise was [detail]."
- "Great question on [specific part]. For [segment], what's worked is [specific tactic], because [reason]."

## Replying under your own posts
- Reply to every substantive comment, ideally within the first hour or two, with more than "thanks". Add a detail, ask a follow-up, or tag in someone relevant who would genuinely add value.
- Replies extend the thread, which deepens conversation and adds dwell.
- Pinning a comment (a resource, clarification, or follow-up question) keeps the conversation going (Jasmin Alić, secondhand [B]).
- Never use pods or "engagement groups" to seed comments. LinkedIn removed such groups and warned members in March 2026 [P].

## Saves and sends
- AuthoredUp found one save worth about 5 likes of reach, and van der Blom reports saves and sends were weighted more heavily from late 2025 [S].
- Content that earns saves and sends includes checklists, templates, frameworks, data, and step-by-step carousels.
- A plain "Save this for later" line at the end of a genuinely useful post is fine. Don't make it a demand.

## Posting cadence
| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Buffer, 2M+ posts | 2-5 posts a week gave about +1,182 impressions per post vs 1 a week. 11+ a week gave more again, but few can sustain it. |
| Ordinal, 219k posts | Engagement rate peaks at 3-4 posts a week for personal profiles and 1-2 for company pages. Total reach keeps rising with volume. |
| van der Blom 2025 | 2-3 posts a week with varied formats: up to +120% vs sporadic posting. |

**Default:** 3-4 posts a week from a personal profile, mixing formats (for example, 1 carousel, 1 story, 1 framework or opinion, 1 image post), plus daily commenting. Space posts a day or so apart.

## Timing
Buffer, Hootsuite, and Sprout disagree on exact windows. Generally, weekday working hours in the audience's time zone (Tuesday to Thursday mornings are often cited) are a reasonable start. About 40% of interactions happen on day one [S, Metricool]. Test 2-3 windows for 2-3 weeks and keep the winner.

## A weekly rhythm
- **Mon:** plan 3-4 posts from one theme, and turn the strongest idea into a carousel.
- **Daily:** 15-30 minutes of commenting (3-10 high-quality comments), and replies to your own comments.
- **Post days:** stay around for the first hour to reply.
- **Fri:** check what earned comments and saves, not just likes, and do more of that.
