# What hurts reach on LinkedIn (2026)

## Officially enforced [P]
Sources: LinkedIn News, 2026-03-12 and 2026-03-13.

| Behavior | What LinkedIn does |
|---|---|
| Engagement pods / engagement groups | Groups removed; members warned (Top Voice removal, account restrictions) |
| Automated or AI-tool comments | Hidden from "Most Relevant"; sometimes hidden outside the network; the terms of service restrict automation tools |
| "Comment to agree" / comment-for-resource bait | Reduced distribution |
| Mismatched media (e.g. a video unrelated to its caption) | Reduced distribution |
| Recycled or click-driven posts | Reduced distribution |

## Contested (present the evidence honestly)
| Topic | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **External links in the post body** | Ordinal (900k posts): linked posts reach about 26.5% less on average, with the effect growing 2023→2025. van der Blom: about -18.8% median per in-body link. Saywhat (Q1 2026): 1-3 links +43% [B]. LinkedIn says it doesn't penalize links. **Default:** keep the value in the post and the link in a comment or omit it, and tell the user the evidence is mixed. |
| **Link in first comment** | May also lose reach. No controlled study compares it with an in-body link. |
| **AI-generated text** | No official penalty. In one study of about 9,000 long-form posts, those flagged as likely AI-written got about 45% less engagement [S/B]. Treat it as an audience effect, not an algorithm rule. |
| **Hashtags** | Hashtag feeds were phased out in 2024. Best supported: 2-3 hashtags (small academic study). More looks spammy. |

## Unsupported (don't present as fact)
- "Editing a post kills reach": vendor claims only.
- "Posting more than once in 18-24h gets you penalized": no source found.
- "Tagging people who don't engage penalizes you": no source. It is still poor etiquette, so tag only real participants.
- "Comments must be 10+ words to count": no source. Still, write substantive comments of 15+ words because they work on humans.

## AI-tell checklist
See `hooks-and-writing.md` → "Voice and AI tells". The short version: no corporate filler, no reflexive "It's not X, it's Y", no emoji bullet walls, no restating moral. Use real names, numbers, and moments.
