## Format performance data [S]
| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Socialinsider 2026 (1.3M posts, 16.6k business pages, 2024-2025) | Engagement rate: native documents 7.00% (+14% YoY), multi-image 6.45%, video 6.00%, image 5.30%, text 4.50%, poll 4.20%, external link 3.25%. Video views -36% YoY. |
| AuthoredUp 2025 (personal profiles) | Reach multipliers: polls 1.64×, documents 1.45×, video 1.10×, text 0.88×. |
| Buffer 2026 (52M+ posts) | Carousel median engagement 21.77% vs video 7.35%, image 6.52%. Inflated, because swipes count as engagement. |
| Metricool 2026 (600k posts) | Carousels get about 17× the interactions of images, yet images are posted 6× more often. |
| LigoSocial (134k posts, within-author) | Documents +22% vs the author's own average. Only decks of 11+ pages beat the author's average (+16% at 11-15 pages, +26% at 16+). |
| SEOWorks | Engagement by slide count: 7-9 slides 53.6%, 4-6 slides 50.4%, 10+ slides 45.5%, 3 or fewer 36.9%. |
| van der Blom 2025 | Native vertical video under 60s +69%. Images with faces up to +50%. Text-only engagement -18%. |

**How to read this:** across studies, documents and carousels are the most consistent winners. Cross-study rates aren't comparable, and the within-author lift (about +22%) is the realistic expectation.

## Document / PDF carousels
**Specs**
- PDF upload, 1080×1350 (4:5, recommended) or 1080×1080. Use one ratio for the whole deck.
- Max 300 pages and 100 MB. Aim for under 10 MB.
- Keep text about 80 px inside every edge (safe zone).
- Type sizes: headline 60-90 px, body 36-48 px (never under 32), footer 28 px.

**Slide count**
- Engagement peaks at 7-9 slides (SEOWorks). Longer decks of 11-16 do best relative to the author's own average (LigoSocial).
- Default: 8-10 for a list or how-to, 11-14 for a deep framework.
- Completion drops past about 12, so put the payoff early.

**Anatomy**
1. **Cover:** a specific outcome promise ("How we cut churn 31% in 90 days"), the biggest type in the deck, the author's face or name, and a swipe cue (→).
2. **Slide 2:** the problem or the stakes, or "what you'll get".
3. **Body:** one idea per slide, a 3-8 word headline plus 15-30 words of support (25-40 total). Use a repeating structure (numbered steps, or problem → insight → fix).
4. **Second-to-last:** proof (a result, chart, or quote).
5. **Last:** a recap line and exactly one CTA (follow, comment, or save). Don't add a second ask such as "Save this. Which one are you fixing?". The question belongs in the caption.

**Design**
- Consistent fonts and colours, high contrast, and simple visuals.
- No dense paragraphs: readers spend 1-2 seconds per slide.

**Caption:** a full post with a fold hook, 2-4 lines of context, and a question. Don't just write "Swipe →".

## Video
- **Evidence conflicts.** van der Blom reports +69% for short vertical video. Socialinsider and Digiday report views falling 36% YoY in late 2025. Dwell normalization means a video competes with other videos.
- **When to use it:** when the user is comfortable on camera, the idea benefits from a face or a demo, and they can post consistently.
- **Shape:** vertical, under about 60-90 s, captions burned in (most people watch muted), a hook in the first 2-3 seconds that matches the caption, and one point.
- **Avoid mismatched media.** LinkedIn now downranks video that doesn't fit its caption (Mar 2026).

## Polls
- High reach multiplier (1.64×), but low engagement rate (4.20%) and low trust when overused.
- Use at most about once a month, with a real question the user will follow up on ("I'll share the results Friday").
- Always add context in the post text, plus a comment prompt ("Pick one and tell me why in the comments").

## Images
- One strong image (a face, a whiteboard, a real screenshot, or a chart) raises engagement over text-only.
- Multi-image posts perform close to documents (6.45%).
- Avoid generic stock photos and AI art that adds nothing.

## Newsletters and articles
- Long-form articles and newsletters reach subscribers by notification. Use them for depth, then promote with a native post that delivers one standalone insight from the piece.
- There's no reliable comparison of their feed reach against regular posts.
