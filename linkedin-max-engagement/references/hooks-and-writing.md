# Hooks, post skeletons, and writing style

Templates are creator-playbook material **[B]**. Formatting and length data come from vendor studies **[S]**. Always fill templates with the user's real specifics and never ship them verbatim.

## The "see more" fold
- About 140-210 characters show on mobile and about 250 on desktop before "…see more" [B, Taplio 2026]. Sources disagree, and LinkedIn doesn't publish a figure, so design for about 140.
- The first line should work alone. Statement openers succeeded more often than question openers (42% vs 35%), and first lines of 1-10 words beat lines of 11+ [S, ContentIn, 200k posts].
- Don't leave a blank line above the cut, don't start with "I'm excited/thrilled/humbled to announce", and don't open with a long setup.
- Line 2 should be a "rehook" that raises the stakes or opens a loop, giving the reader a reason to click "see more" (Jasmin Alić's hook/rehook idea, secondhand [B]).

## Hook templates
**Story / personal**
- "[N] years ago, I [failed at / struggled with] [specific thing]." (Lara Acosta's SLAY, via Buldrr)
- "I used to [old habit]. Then [specific moment] happened."
- "A client came to me with [specific problem]."
- "This week, I [unusual event with a number]." (Kleo)
- "I was always [relatable flaw] at work." (Matt Barker style, secondhand)

**Authority / data**
- "We went from [A] to [B] in [time]. Here's what actually moved it:"
- "[Concept 1] = [outcome 1]. [Concept 2] = [outcome 2]." (Kleo, Codie Sanchez style)
- "[Surprising stat that names the reader's problem]." (Taplio; verify the stat)
- "The [N]-step process I use to [result] in under [time]."

**Contrarian / stance**
- "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's what actually works:" (Taplio)
- "Don't [common action]. Do [alternative] instead." (Kleo)
- "Your [role/habit] is a trap." (Justin Welsh style, secondhand)
- "Unpopular opinion: [defensible claim]."

**List / open loop**
- "Here's my [N]-step playbook to [outcome]:" (Adam Robinson's hook levers, via Catalyst)
- "[Past win], but [new challenge]:" (story plus an open loop that ends in a colon)
- "[N] things I wish I knew before [milestone]:"
- "Why does [pain point] keep happening?" (Taplio. Question openers drive comments but trail statement openers on overall success.)

## Post skeletons
**SLAY** (Lara Acosta [B])
- **S**tory: a short personal moment, 2-5 lines, with one concrete detail.
- **L**esson: one sentence ("That taught me…").
- **A**ctionable: 3-5 practical steps.
- **Y**ou: a direct question to the reader.

**Story → lesson**
```
[Hook: the moment, ≤10 words]
[Rehook: the stakes]

[Scene: 2-4 short lines, a number or a name]
[The turn: "That's when I realised…"]

[Lesson, one line]

[Question tied to the reader's experience]
```

**Listicle**
```
[N] [things] I learned [doing X]:

1. [Item]: [one line]
2. [Item]: [one line]
…

[Which one would you add / disagree with?]
```

**Contrarian**
Stance → the common belief and why it fails (2 lines) → evidence or experience (3-4 lines) → nuance (when the belief *is* right) → a question inviting disagreement.

**Framework (PAS)**
Problem → agitate (the cost of ignoring it) → a named framework with N steps, one line each → a short example → question.

**Case study**
"[Client] had [problem]. [N] weeks later, [result]." → what we did (3 steps) → the one thing that mattered → question.

**Behind the scenes**
"This week, I [specific action]." → what went right or wrong → what you'd change → question.

Welsh's spoke formats translate well to LinkedIn: Story (PAIPS), Observation, Contrarian, Listicle, and Past-vs-Present (Old way / New way).

## Formatting and length (data)
| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 125-300 words had the best success rate (44%). Under 25 words: 38%. 25-100 words: 27%. | ContentIn, 200k posts [S] |
| Engagement by length in characters: 0-200: 1.53%; 200-500: 1.88%; 1,000-2,000: 2.23%; 2,000+: 2.56% | Taplio, Mar 2026 data [S, vendor] |
| Fewer than 3 line breaks: 28% success | ContentIn [S] |
| Bold or lists: 8% engagement vs 4% plain | ContentIn [S] |
| Image with the post: 2.77% vs 1.98% text-only | Taplio [S] |
| A question in the post: +77% comments | Metricool, 600k posts [S] |
| Max post length: 3,000 characters | platform limit |

**Rules of thumb**
- Keep paragraphs to one or two sentences, and lines to about 40-60 characters.
- Write at a 7th-8th grade reading level.
- Use real numbered lists, not decorative emoji bullets.
- Use bold sparingly, for the key line.
- The two length findings reconcile: a tight 125-300 word post is the safe default, and long posts work when they are dense with value.

## Closers
**Good:**
- "What's the one [X] you'd add?"
- "Which of these have you seen fail?"
- "How does your team handle [specific situation]?"
- "[Option A] or [option B]: which would you pick and why?"

**Bad:**
- "Agree?"
- "Thoughts?"
- "Comment YES / 🔥 and I'll send it to you": officially downranked comment bait
- "Like if you agree"

If the user wants to give away a resource, offer it plainly ("Link to the template in the comments"), not as a reward for commenting.

## Voice and AI tells
In a single study of about 9,000 long-form posts, posts flagged as likely AI-written got about 45% less engagement [S/B, Originality.ai]. LinkedIn has announced no AI penalty, but people scroll past slop, and that shows up as lower dwell.

Cut these:
- "In today's fast-paced world" / "In an ever-evolving landscape"
- "I'm thrilled to announce" (unless it really is an announcement, and even then lead with the substance)
- "Let that sink in." / "Read that again." / "Here's the kicker:"
- The negation-contrast pattern in every form: "It's not about X. It's about Y.", "Not X. Y.", "X wasn't the problem. Y was." Models produce this constantly even when told to avoid the literal phrase, so search for not/isn't/wasn't and rewrite each one as a direct claim.
- "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "navigate", "delve", "elevate", "synergy"
- emoji bullets on every line, rocket emojis
- a moral at the end that restates the whole post
- three-item rhythm everywhere

Replace them with names, numbers, sensory details, and the user's own phrasing.
