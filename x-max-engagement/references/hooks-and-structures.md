## Hook principles
- **Be clear, not clever.** Make it obvious WHO it's for, WHAT they get, and WHY it matters now (Ship 30).
- **Add "voltage" through specificity.** A specific who, what, feeling, and promise beats a generic topic (Nicolas Cole).
- **Lead with a question many people already have**, not with the topic name (Cole).
- **The lead-in is the most important thing you write**: a headline plus a one-sentence opener (Cole, Dickie Bush).
- **Twist the knife.** Name the reader's pain or cost precisely (Ship 30).
- **Ground it in the weights.** The hook earns the click-dwell (0.4) and the stop. Shareability and replies come from the body and the closer.

## Hook templates
Fill the brackets with real numbers and specifics.

**Contrarian / reframe**
- "You don't need [popular thing]. You need [real thing]." (Typefully)
- "[Topic] is not about [common belief]. It's about [actual driver]." (Typefully)
- "Hard truth about [topic]:" (Typefully)
- "Everyone says [advice]. The data says [opposite]." (adapted)
- "Old way: [x]. New way: [y]." (Justin Welsh "Old Way / New Way")

**Proof / result**
- "I [did hard thing] [N] times. Most people make the same [N] mistakes. Here's how to avoid them:" (Tweet Hunter #10)
- "Over the last [period], [person/company] gained [number]. I spent [effort] learning how. Here's their [N]-part framework:" (Ship 30)
- "I've [read/watched/tested] [big number] [things] in [time]. These [N] actually [result]:" (Tweet Hunter #4)
- "[Before state]. [After state]. What changed:" (before/after)

**Audience call-out**
- "If you're a [identity] in your [age range/stage], read this:" (Ship 30)
- "If you're under [age] and want [desire] by [age], this is the playbook:" (Tweet Hunter #9)
- "[Role]s: which of these [N] [things] do you actually use?" (reply-oriented)

**Curiosity / story**
- "I tried [thing]. The result? [Bad outcome]. Until [one change]." (Tweet Hunter #1)
- "When [event] happened in [year], [surprising consequence]." (Ship 30 "moment in time")
- "[Institution] never taught me [skill]. So I learned it the hard way. Here it is for free:" (Ship 30)
- "I was wrong about [belief] for [N] years. Here's what changed my mind:" (admission)

**Resource / list**
- "[N] free [resources] to [desire], from [credible sources]:" (Tweet Hunter #3)
- "How to [outcome that feels hard] without [usual cost]:" (Tweet Hunter #7)
- "[Niche] 101. I've [proof] over [time]. Here's the exact process:" (Tweet Hunter #8)
- "[Niche skill] is a superpower. Learn it and you can [desire]. [N] ways to start:" (Tweet Hunter #6)

**Engagement prompts** (single-post, aimed at replies)
- "In one word, describe [thing your audience knows well]." (Typefully)
- "What's one [tool/habit/rule] in [niche] you'd never give up?"
- "[Statement]. Change my mind."

Keep the opener short. Under about 100 characters is a good target, and Ordinal's data shows 100-200 character posts with the highest engagement rate [S].

## Single-post skeletons
**Listicle**
```
[N] [things] that [outcome]:

1. [Item]: [one-line why]
2. [Item]: [one-line why]
3. [Item]: [one-line why]

[One-line takeaway or question]
```
**Contrarian**
```
[Common belief] is wrong.

[What's actually true], in one line.

[2-3 short lines of evidence, a number, or your experience]

[Debatable closing question]
```
**Story (Welsh's PAIPS)**
Pain → Agitate (the cost) → Intrigue (surprising detail) → Positive future → Solution (one line).

**Lesson**
```
I [did X] [N] times.
The result: [specific outcome].
The lesson: [one sentence].
```
**What / How / Why (Cole)**
Line 1 = the what. 3-5 bullets = the how. Last sentence = the why.

## Thread anatomy
1. **Post 1 (the lead-in):** a specific promise and credibility ("I did X for N years. Here are the N lessons:"). End it with a reason to open the thread (`↓`, "Here's how:").
2. **Post 2:** reinforce the hook with the most striking number or detail. Many readers decide here.
3. **Body:** one idea per post, numbered. End posts with an open loop ("Here's where it gets interesting:"). Dickie Bush's example thread had open loops on 14 of 18 posts.
4. **Recap:** 2-3 lines summarizing the list. This recap is the most copyable part, which targets the copy-link weight.
5. **Closer:** a single CTA, either follow for more on [topic] or a question. Optionally reply to your last post with a link back to post 1.
6. **Length:** 5-12 posts is the usual range. Sources conflict, and none have data. Cut any post that doesn't earn its place.

**Test first** (Dickie Bush): post the core idea as a single post. If it gets early replies, expand it into a thread or an Article.

Buffer's older thread experiment found engagement peaks on the first post and spikes again on the last, so put the most effort into those two.

## Quote-post shapes
- **Add data:** "This matches what we saw: [number from your experience]."
- **Extend:** "Good list. The one most people miss: [item + why]."
- **Respectful counter:** "I'd push back on [specific point]. In [context], [counter-evidence]."
- **Apply:** "How this plays out for [your niche]: [2-3 lines]."

Never quote a post just to dunk on it, because predicted mutes and not-interested marks cost far more than the quote earns.

## X Articles
- Open to all Premium subscribers since January 2026 (secondary reports). The limit is about 25,000 characters (sources conflict on higher Premium+ limits), and only about the first 280 characters show before "Show more" [B, Tweet Hunter].
- **Use an Article** for depth that would be a 15+ post thread, evergreen guides, or case studies.
- **Structure:** title and first 280 characters work like a hook → short sections with headers → examples and visuals → one CTA. Writing it as a thread first and then unrolling it into an Article works well.
- Promote the Article with a native post that delivers one standalone insight from it. Don't post a bare link.
- There is no primary data comparing the reach of Articles and threads.

## Closers (reply magnets)
**Good** (specific, answerable in one line):
- "Which of these do you disagree with?"
- "What would you add as #[N+1]?"
- "What's the hardest part of [X] for you?"
- "[Option A] or [option B]? Why?"

**Bad:** "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Like if…", "RT if…", "Comment [word] and I'll DM you…". The last is engagement solicitation, which X said in March 2026 it is building Grok detection for. It also cheapens the brand.
