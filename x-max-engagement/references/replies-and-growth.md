## Why replies still matter
- Your reply on someone else's post is your content shown to their audience. That makes it the main way small accounts get discovered.
- Replies under your own posts feed the reply prediction (weight 5, or 20 for mutual follows) of the post they sit under.
- Out-of-network replies are discounted, and replies from non-followers often sit behind "show more replies". So one reply that adds value beats many generic ones.
- Data: Buffer's 2026 study found that accounts that reply to others get about 8% more engagement on their own posts. Buffer calls this its least certain result [S].

## Writing a high-value reply
- **Add one new thing:** a specific number, a counter-example, your own result, a tool, or a sharper framing.
- **Keep it to 1-3 short sentences.** Under a big post, brevity wins.
- **Don't start with praise.** "Great post!" adds nothing. If you agree, say why with a specific.
- **End with a light hook when it fits:** a question to the author or a "curious if…".
- **Write in your own voice, specific to the post.** Templated or automated replies are what X's 2026 reply-bot ban waves target.

Templates (fill in the real specifics):
- "We tested this on [N] [things]: [result]. The surprise was [detail]."
- "Counterpoint from [context]: [specific case] where [thing] didn't hold."
- "The step most people skip here is [X]. Without it, [consequence]."
- "Adding to #[n]: [specific tool, number, or example]."

## Choosing where to reply
- Reply in your niche, so the people who see your reply are the people you want following you.
- Pick posts that are recent and gaining traction, where an early, high-value reply is likely to stay visible. Vendors claim speed matters (for example, "10 replies in 15 min beat 10 over 24 h"), but this is unverified [B].
- Mix account sizes. Peers and smaller accounts reply back and follow, which builds mutuals. Larger accounts bring reach when your reply is excellent.
- Skip threads you'd only be piling onto, and skip fights.

## Replying under your own posts
- Reply to substantive replies with substance: add a detail, ask a follow-up, or give credit. Don't post a bare "thanks!".
- This keeps the conversation alive and grows the reply count the model sees.
- Pin a reply with the link, a resource, or a follow-up when useful.

## The mutual-follow loop
Since July 2026, a reply from a mutual follow on your original post scores 20 instead of 5 (see algorithm.md).
- Follow back people in your niche who reply thoughtfully.
- Engage with their posts too, because mutual relationships compound.
- Don't mass-follow strangers. Follow churn looks spammy and doesn't create the conversations that give the boost its value.

## Daily rhythm (a starting point, not a rule)
- **1-3 strong original posts a day,** spaced hours apart. Author diversity discounts bunched posts, and cold start helps originals under 2 hours old.
- **10-30 thoughtful replies a day** in your niche. Early-stage accounts can go heavier on replies than on posts. X's product lead said low-value replies use up reach, and you don't need hundreds.
- **Answer your own replies.** For a post that's gaining traction, spend most of the first couple of hours there.
- **Weekly:** turn your best-performing single post into a thread or Article, and drop formats that consistently flop.
- **Vary formats** (text, image, thread, quote) so the account doesn't look automated.

## Premium
- Buffer's 2025 study of 18.8M posts: the non-subscriber median was under 100 impressions per post, Premium above 600, and Premium+ above 1,550. This is correlation, not causation [S].
- Premium also unlocks long posts and Articles, and Original Content Rewards (the creator payouts that replaced revenue sharing in Aug-Sep 2026) requires it.
- If the user is serious about growth on X and isn't Premium, flag this as the biggest single lever, honestly framed as correlation.

## What changed in 2026 (update old advice)
- **Communities:** shut down on 2026-05-30, so drop any "post in Communities" advice.
- **API:** programmatic replies are now limited to posts that mention or quote the author (Feb 2026), and apps that pay users for posting are banned (Jan 2026).
- **Bots:** reply-bot ban waves ran in April 2026.
- **Engagement bait:** in March 2026 X said Grok-based detection of engagement solicitation was being built.
